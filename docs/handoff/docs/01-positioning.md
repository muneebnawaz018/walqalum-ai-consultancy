# Positioning — why the site says what it says

## The problem with the old site

It did not fail because it looked bad. It failed because of one structural
choice: **the services were named after technologies instead of outcomes.**

"Drupal Development." "Shopify Development." "Mobile Application Development."

That is a menu, and menus are for vendors. A buyer reading a menu asks "how much
per hour?" — and the About page answered, out loud: *$25/hour, Affordable
Excellence.*

Consultancies never publish a rate. They publish a **diagnosis**. The buyer
arrives worried about something, sees the worry named precisely, and concludes
that whoever wrote it has seen this before. Price becomes a later conversation
with a different anchor.

## The architecture

**Four practices**, named for outcomes: AI & Data Advisory · Digital
Transformation · Growth & Demand Systems · Engineering & Platforms.

**Three diagnostics** — named, time-boxed, priced before they start. This is the
single element that most separates a consultancy from a vendor: vendors quote
projects, consultancies sell diagnostics that lead into programmes.

**Eight capability pages** sitting under the practices. These are the old service
pages, kept because they are indexed and rank for commercial searches. The
practice sells the firm to a CEO; the capability catches someone searching
"Shopify development company Dubai" with a budget already approved.

## Removed deliberately

`$25/hour` · "Affordable Excellence" · "IT Startup" · "95% success rate" ·
"85% cost recovery rate" · technology names as top-level services.

## Voice

British English. Problem first, build last. No "leverage", "cutting-edge",
"empower", "seamless". Willing to say when something is the wrong purchase.
Never a claim that cannot be evidenced.
